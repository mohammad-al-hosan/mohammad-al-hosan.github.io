Design and Simulation of a Class-A Common-Emitter RF Power Amplifier for the 433 MHz ISM Band

A single-stage NPN BJT design in Cadence Virtuoso, with LC impedance matching and comprehensive RF performance analysis

This project develops a 433 MHz Class-A common-emitter RF power amplifier using the open gpdk090 NPN BJT model in Cadence Virtuoso. A passive bias network establishes the DC operating point, while lumped-element LC networks match the amplifier to a nominal 50 Ω source and load. The design is examined from small-signal impedance and stability through harmonic-balance power sweeps, load-pull, third-order intermodulation and noise analysis.

At 433 MHz, the simulated design reaches a calculated power gain of 23.42 dB, with input/output return losses near −13.4 dB. Large-signal analysis reports 17.03 dBm saturated output power, 13.90 dBm at 1 dB compression, and 42.3% peak power-added efficiency.

Scope: Cadence Virtuoso / Spectre schematic-level simulations using a 90 nm PDK BJT model. This is not a fabricated, measured or post-layout RF amplifier.

Transistor and operating point

The RF stage employs the gpdk090 NPN BJT (PDK version 4.6), with a 0.8 μm emitter size and a device multiplier of 10. A 25 Ω emitter resistor establishes an approximately 0.657 V emitter drop; the resulting quiescent collector current is about 24.59 mA.

The study retained VCE,Q = 8.884 V rather than forcing the nominal 6 V supply midpoint: bias shifts toward 6 V without redesigning the associated networks reduced gain, saturation power and PAE in simulation.

Biasing and coupling network

Base and collector RF chokes isolate the DC paths from RF signals. Capacitors support supply decoupling and emitter bypass, while input/output LC sections are integrated in the complete circuit.

Method: Cadence Virtuoso schematic with Spectre simulations through ADE L; gpdk090_bipolar.scs, highperf_bjt_s model section.

02

Input and output impedance matching

Measure the complex port impedances, design LC networks, and verify at 433 MHz.

Before matching

The unmatched stage presents approximately Zin = 224.38 − j2.436 Ω and Zout = 3147.35 − j951.09 Ω at 433 MHz. S-parameter results and Smith-chart transformations were used to select lumped matching networks for a nominal 50 Ω environment.

  • Input: series inductor and shunt capacitor.
  • Output: shunt capacitor followed by a series inductor.
  • Matching is evaluated at the operating frequency rather than claimed as broadband.

After matching

The reported post-matching impedances at 433 MHz are approximately 68.21 Ω at the input and 37.18 Ω at the output. They are closer to 50 Ω than the unmatched complex impedances; the S-parameter return losses in the next section provide a separate measure of port reflections.

This is a lumped LC matching network simulated at the schematic level. PCB trace parasitics, fabricated component tolerances and post-layout effects were not characterized.

Gain and usable frequency span

At 433 MHz, S21 is 23.2187 dB and the calculated power gain is 23.423 dB. The paper reports a −3 dB frequency span from 379.585 to 490.359 MHz (approximately 111 MHz).

The gain response peaks near the intended ISM operating frequency, rather than exhibiting a wide flat passband.

Matching and reverse isolation

Both port reflection coefficients are near −13.4 dB at 433 MHz, consistent with reasonably low reflected power at the operating frequency. Reverse transmission S12 = −68.8505 dB indicates strong output-to-input isolation.

These results are small-signal quantities; large-signal gain compression and efficiency are assessed separately by harmonic-balance simulations.

The paper verifies the conventional two-port unconditional-stability criteria K > 1 and |Δ| < 1 over the examined frequency sweep. It reports K ≈ 115.72 and |Δ| ≈ 0.0256 at 433 MHz; it does not establish an equivalent guarantee under every possible large-signal operating condition.

Complementary stability checks

The K/|Δ| curves provide the frequency-domain test; the load stability boundary (LSB) and source stability boundary (SSB) show stable/unstable impedance regions on a Smith-chart representation at 433 MHz. The paper reports both boundaries outside the passive Smith-chart region.

Load-pull optimization

Large-signal load-pull sweeps complex load impedances to identify a high-output-power region. At the intrinsic transistor output plane, the paper reports Zopt ≈ 0.6915 − j0.172 Ω. This is not the external 50 Ω system impedance; the LC output network transforms the environment seen by the transistor.

Figure 9(a) shows constant-power contours, while 9(b) lists the associated simulated contour power levels, approximately −4.21 to 18.29 dBm.

Harmonic-balance simulation

Input power was swept from −40 to +20 dBm in 0.1 dB steps. The paper also plots the harmonic output spectrum at a fixed 1 dBm input.

At that input level, the reported voltage spectral amplitudes are 2.22 V for the fundamental and 326.98 / 63.14 mV for the next two plotted harmonics. The corresponding output powers are 16.92, 0.290 and −13.99 dBm.

Gain compression and saturation

The simulated output-power sweep gives a 1 dB compression point of 13.90 dBm and a saturated output power of 17.033 dBm. The latter corresponds to about 50.5 mW at the output.

These levels are below the output powers of the selected hardware-validated BJT reference amplifiers cited in the paper; the reported design places greater emphasis on a low quiescent current in a reproducible open-PDK simulation.

Power-added efficiency

The PAE curve reaches a reported maximum of 42.30%, versus 33.23% at the reported saturated operating point. The paper uses a total DC consumption of 315.34 mW for its PAE calculation:

PAE = (Pout − Pin) / PDC × 100%

PAE penalizes both DC power consumption and RF input power, unlike collector/drain efficiency. Values here are simulated and depend on the PDK model and idealized schematic-level passive network.

Two-tone IMD3 test

Two equal-power tones at 432.9 MHz and 433.1 MHz generate third-order intermodulation products at 432.7 MHz and 433.3 MHz. With −20 dBm input per tone, the paper reports a fundamental output level of 2.159 dBm and an IMD3 level of −35.526 dBm.

Using the reported two-tone results and third-order extrapolation gives IIP3 = −1.157 dBm, consistent with the Cadence IPN result of approximately −1.16 dBm. This is a quantified linearity metric, not a claim of distortion-free operation.

Intercept interpretation and noise

The IPN plot extrapolates the fundamental and third-order terms to estimate their intercept. Its value is calculated from low-level two-tone behavior rather than measured directly at the extrapolated intersection.

The paper also reports 16.16 dB noise figure at 433 MHz from a 100 MHz–1 GHz noise sweep. No separate noise-figure plot was included in the published figures, so the page reports the value as text rather than inventing a curve.

What this study establishes

  • A complete schematic-level design workflow for a 433 MHz Class-A PA, including bias, matching, small-signal behavior and stability.
  • An explicit large-signal RF characterization using load-pull, harmonic balance, compression and PAE.
  • A two-tone intermodulation result and noise figure, documenting limitations beyond the nominal forward-gain figure.
  • A reproducible open-PDK reference design, not an experimental or post-layout demonstration. Layout-aware parasitics, hardware prototyping and multi-stage topologies remain future extensions.
