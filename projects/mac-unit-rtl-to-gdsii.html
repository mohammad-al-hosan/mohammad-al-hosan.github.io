RTL-to-GDSII Implementation of an Optimised Multiply-Accumulate Unit

Cadence GPDK045 (45 nm) and OpenROAD IHP SG13G2 (130 nm) cross-toolchain validation

A parameterized 16-bit multiply-accumulate unit taken from SystemVerilog through synthesis, place-and-route and DRC-clean layout in Cadence GPDK045, then re-implemented independently in OpenROAD on IHP SG13G2.

Workflow

STEP 1RTL design16-bit operands, 40-bit accumulator, ready-valid handshake, 3-stage datapath
STEP 2Verification8 directed tests, then constrained-random with 288 cross-coverage bins
STEP 3Synthesis sweepsFrequency, input delay, output delay; power, area, power-area-period
STEP 4Physical designFloorplan, power mesh, placement, CTS, routing
STEP 5Die compression53 → 47 µm until timing closure fails
STEP 6OpenROAD checkSame RTL on IHP 130 nm with Yosys/ABC

Key findings

  1. Constraints do not respond alikeInput-delay power and area have an interior minimum at 2 ns. Output delay rises monotonically from its minimum at 0.1 ns.
  2. 100 MHz minimises power-area-periodArea stays flat below 100 MHz and rises sharply above it as larger, faster cells are inserted.
  3. Same RTL, different netlistsCadence maps the multiplier to a carry-save adder tree (778 cells); OpenROAD gives 1,847 cells.

Note

Sequential tuning worked here, likely because the design is small; a joint sweep is future work. Coverage at 100% means every defined bin was hit, not that the design is exhaustively verified. The two flows use different processes, libraries and placement densities, so cell counts and utilisation are not a measure of relative tool quality. Only the nominal PVT corner was studied.
