Sequential Electrostatic Engineering in 20 nm DG-MOSFETs

A systematic investigation into mitigating drain-induced barrier lowering (DIBL) via step-oxide, dual-material gate, and halo doping without relying on uniform, tunneling-unsafe oxide scaling.

1 UO-DG Baseline

The Oxide Scaling Limit

To establish a baseline, the uniform gate oxide (top and bottom HfO2) was swept from 2.0 nm down to 0.8 nm. While the 0.8 nm device exhibits excellent electrostatic control, analytical WKB approximation reveals the tunneling probability increases ~280×, rendering it unviable for low-power applications.

The 2.0 nm device remains safe from gate tunneling but suffers from severe short-channel effects, establishing our engineering target: 67.21 mV/V DIBL.

2 SO-DG

Geometric Decoupling

To retain tunneling safety at the source while improving electrostatic control, an asymmetric oxide was introduced. Keeping the source-side oxide at a safer 2.0 nm, the drain-side oxide was scaled down to 0.8 nm.

This configuration confines the drain-induced potential perturbation largely to the drain side. By decoupling the source and drain electrostatics, the Step-Oxide (SO-DG) design achieves an 18% reduction in DIBL compared to the baseline, at the cost of a minor fT penalty.

3 DMSO-DG

Barrier Modulation via Dual-Metal

Further DIBL reduction requires direct manipulation of the channel potential. A dual-material gate (1:1 split) was applied over the SO-DG structure. Using a higher work-function metal (M1) near the source forms an abrupt step in the conduction band, elevating the source-side barrier against drain influence.

A work-function difference (ΔΦ) of 0.3 eV yielded the strongest DIBL suppression (cutting it to 32.95 mV/V), though it introduced a distinct trade-off, lowering drive current by 17%.

4 Halo-DMSO-DG

Depletion Width Confinement

Despite the DMG implementation, the lightly doped channel still permits the drain depletion region to expand toward the source under high drain bias. Localized heavily-doped P+ halos were introduced at the junctions to limit this expansion.

While a concentration of 1×1019 cm-3 provided the absolute lowest leakage, the 2×1018 cm-3 configuration was selected as the optimal engineering trade-off. It secures massive DIBL reduction without severely degrading the cutoff frequency (fT) or unnecessarily shifting the threshold voltage.

Quantitative Progression

The sequential application of these three techniques quantifies the "cost" of electrostatic improvements. The step oxide is a low-cost intervention. The dual-material gate provides the dominant DIBL cut but costs drive current. The balanced halo minimizes leakage without sacrificing high-frequency performance.
