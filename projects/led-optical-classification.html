Optical Quality Analysis and Classification of LEDs Using KNN and Random Forest Models

A low-cost tester (about 50 USD) that grades LEDs into nine colour and quality classes

A closed-loop constant-current driver, a BPW34 photodiode and a TCS34725 colour sensor feed a Python pipeline that turns raw counts into eleven physical features, which simple classifiers then use to sort LEDs by colour and quality tier.

Driver circuit

Why a constant-current driver

  • Why it matters: a series resistor would let each LED's forward voltage change its current, so brightness would reflect the supply, not the device.
  • The op-amp forces the sense-resistor voltage to match the PWM reference, so the current is set by I = Vref/Rs: 10 mA and 30 mA.
  • An RC filter (4.7 kΩ, 10 µF) turns the 490 Hz PWM into a smooth DC reference.
  • Simulated before building: current held within 1% over a 1.8 to 3.4 V forward-voltage range.

Key findings

  1. Two features carry nearly everythingSlope efficiency with dominant wavelength gives 99.5% with KNN, against 99.5% for all eleven.
  2. Classes are well separatedMinimum centroid distance is 1.68 standard deviations; logistic regression already reaches 95.4%.
  3. Firmware does no physicsEvery calibration constant, including the 15-point photodiode table, is editable at runtime without reflashing.
